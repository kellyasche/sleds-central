Multivariate Confirmation Analysis: Disconnection Persistence Among Non–Postsecondary Graduates

Introduction

The bivariate analysis of workforce disconnection among non-college graduates identified two distinct disconnection mechanisms operating within a single combined measure. Non-sustained employment (working, but below the sustained-employment threshold) and absence of any Minnesota employment record behave as structurally different phenomena: the former is front-loaded and largely resolves within five years, while the latter is bimodal and behaves as a durable end-state once established. Separating these two mechanisms revealed that different demographic and high-school-accomplishment variables predict each one — most notably, Special Education status and English learner status appeared to drive no-record persistence specifically, while Economic status and Gender appeared to drive non-sustained persistence specifically, and different racial/ethnic groups showed disparities concentrated in one mechanism or the other.

This multivariate analysis exists to confirm whether that mechanism-split pattern holds once all variables are considered simultaneously, rather than one at a time. This step is necessary because earlier work in this project has shown that bivariate relationships — particularly those involving English learner status — can substantially attenuate or shift once other variables, especially missing-data patterns in academic assessment scores, are accounted for in the same model. Before this mechanism-split finding is presented as a confirmed result, it should be tested under the same conditions.

Methodology

Study Population

The analytic sample is restricted to individuals who did not complete a postsecondary credential, consistent with the non-college-graduate population used throughout this project. The sample is further restricted to individuals who graduated (or exited postsecondary attendance, for those who attended without completing) after 2010, consistent with the cohort restriction applied elsewhere in this project to avoid the PSEO participation tracking gap present in earlier cohorts.

Dependent Variables

This analysis models two outcomes, each derived from the disconnection-persistence categories established in the bivariate analysis. Each graduate’s workforce status was tracked across the first five years after exiting formal education (high school for direct-to-workforce graduates, or postsecondary attendance for those who attended without completing).

  • Persistent non-sustained employment — binary (1 = non-sustained workforce participation in 4 or 5 of the first 5 years, 0 = otherwise). This isolates the instability mechanism identified in the bivariate work.
  • Persistent no-record status — binary (1 = no Minnesota employment record in 4 or 5 of the first 5 years, 0 = otherwise). This isolates the disappearance-from-the-system mechanism identified in the bivariate work.

Each outcome is modeled separately, in its own specification, rather than as levels of a single combined variable. This mirrors the logic of the bivariate analysis: combining the two mechanisms into one model would risk re-obscuring the distinction this analysis is specifically designed to confirm.

Independent Variables

The following indepdent variables were confirmed associated to our dependent variables in the bivariate analysis. These will all be considered for use in the modeling.

Demographics

  • Gender
  • RaceEthnicity
  • economic.status
  • SpecialEdStatus
  • english.learner

High school characteristics

  • edr
  • avg.unemp.rate
  • avg.wages.pct.state

High school accomplishments

  • pseo.participant
  • ap.exam
  • took.ACT
  • ACTCompositeScore (recoded to 0 for non-test-takers, entered alongside took.ACT, per the missing-indicator approach established earlier in this project)
  • cte.achievement
  • total.cte.courses.taken
  • sat.taken

Postsecondary variable

  • attended.ps — included specifically because it was the single strongest predictor of overall disconnection in the bivariate analysis, and it is the one variable found to meaningfully affect both disconnection mechanisms rather than only one.

Modeling Approach

Each outcome (persistent non-sustained status, persistent no-record status) is modeled using a staged block-entry sequence, consistent with the modeling structure used throughout this project’s other multivariate confirmation analyses. Predictors are introduced in four stages to make attenuation of any individual variable’s effect visible and attributable to a specific block, rather than only observable in a single final specification.

Model 1: Demographics

  • Demographics: Gender, RaceEthnicity, economic.status, SpecialEdStatus, english.learner, non.english.home

Model 2: add High School Characteristics

  • High school characteristics: edr, avg.unemp.rate, avg.wages.pct.state

Model 3: Add High School Accomplishments

  • Adds: pseo.participant, took.ACT, ACTCompositeScore.filled, ap.exam, cte.achievement, total.cte.courses.taken, sat.taken

Model 4: Add Postsecondary Attempt

  • Adds: attended.ps

Each model is compared to the prior one using a likelihood ratio test to confirm it adds significant explanatory power, with AIC tracked across the sequence to summarize overall model improvement. This staged structure is applied identically to both outcomes, allowing direct comparison of how each mechanism’s predictors evolve across the same three blocks.

Evaluation Criteria

Results will be reported using odds ratios, 95% confidence intervals, and statistical significance levels. Interpretation will focus specifically on whether the demographic variables identified as mechanism-specific in the bivariate analysis (Special Education and English learner status for no-record persistence; Economic status and Gender for non-sustained persistence; racial/ethnic groups for whichever mechanism their bivariate disparity was concentrated in) remain significant, attenuate, or reverse once the full variable set is included. Findings will be described as associations holding other included variables constant. No causal claims will be made.


Prepping the data

The modeling dataset begins from the merged file containing both the disconnection-persistence categories (established in the bivariate analysis) and the full set of demographic, high school, and postsecondary variables used throughout this project. Three preparation steps are applied before modeling.

Cohort restriction. The sample is restricted to graduates from 2011 onward (grad.year > 2010). This mirrors the cohort restriction applied throughout this project’s other analyses of non-college graduates: PSEO participation was not reliably tracked prior to 2011 (missingness runs 92-100% in the 2008-2010 cohorts, dropping to near-zero by 2014), and including those cohorts would risk misclassifying untracked PSEO participants as confirmed non-participants.

Reference level assignment. Categorical variables are releveled to match the reference categories used consistently throughout this project (male, White, non-economically-disadvantaged, non-Special-Education, non-English-learner, EDR 5 - North Central, no CTE involvement, non-participant/non-test-taker for the binary accomplishment variables). This keeps odds ratios in this analysis directly comparable in direction and interpretation to every other model built in this project.

Missing-indicator recodes for two variables with structural or correlated missingness:

  • ACT composite score. Students who did not take the ACT have no composite score by definition — confirmed here to be a fully deterministic relationship with took.ACT (zero mismatches between the two). ACTCompositeScore is recoded to 0 for non-test-takers and entered into the model alongside took.ACT, so that took.ACT absorbs the effect of test participation itself while the filled score captures the achievement gradient among test-takers only.

Dependent variable construction. The two outcomes tested in this analysis are built directly from the bivariate persistence categories, collapsed to binary indicators:

  • persistent.non_sustained — 1 if the graduate was non-sustained in 4 or 5 of their first 5 years post-education-exit, 0 otherwise.

  • persistent.no_record — 1 if the graduate had no Minnesota employment record in 4 or 5 of those years, 0 otherwise.

Bottom line

This confirmatory analysis validated the core mechanism-split hypothesis for four of five demographic variables and for every accomplishment variable tested, with American Indian’s status left genuinely unresolved rather than confirmed either way. The clearest headline finding remains the Black-White gap: unmoved and slightly widening across every block, in a fifth distinct population/outcome combination now confirming this same pattern across the entire project. Summary: Multivariate Confirmation — Persistent No Minnesota Employment Record

Overview

Fit improved substantially and consistently across all four models (AIC: 23,756 → 23,736 → 23,415 → 23,278; pseudo-R²: 0.013 → 0.015 → 0.029 → 0.034), with the accomplishments block and the postsecondary-attendance block both producing the two largest single-block improvements of either sequence in this entire two-part analysis. This confirmatory analysis validated nearly every mechanism-specific prediction from the bivariate stage, with one clear exception and one genuinely new finding worth flagging.

The predictions that held up, several dramatically

  • Non-English home was the largest, most stable coefficient in every single model (OR ranging from 2.10 to 2.23 across all four blocks) — barely moving despite every control added. This is now the single most robust individual finding across this entire two-part analysis, confirming the bivariate result that non-English-home students are dramatically more likely to disappear from Minnesota’s employment record specifically, with essentially no relationship to job instability.
  • English learner status was significant and harmful in every model (OR ≈ 1.25–1.27) — a complete, consistent mirror image of its null role in the non-sustained sequence, confirming EL’s disadvantage operates specifically through the no-record mechanism.
  • Gender was not significant in any of the four models — the complete opposite of its strong, stable role in the non-sustained sequence, confirming this variable’s disadvantage is entirely about job instability and has nothing to do with disappearing from the record.
  • Asian/Pacific Islander and Hispanic were both significant and harmful in every model (OR ranging roughly 1.29–1.38) — a clean reversal from their null role in the non-sustained sequence, directly confirming the bivariate prediction that these two groups’ disadvantage concentrates in the no-record mechanism.
  • American Indian remained significant and harmful throughout, though it attenuated somewhat as HS characteristics and accomplishments were added (OR moving from 1.59 down to 1.38), confirming this group’s disadvantage — unlike Asian/Pacific Islander and Hispanic — operates through both mechanisms.
  • Economic status was significant and protective in every model (OR ≈ 0.77–0.81) — a genuinely new finding not clearly anticipated by the bivariate cross-tab, worth reporting as its own result: economically disadvantaged non-completers appear less likely to disappear from Minnesota’s employment record, plausibly reflecting fewer resources to relocate or disengage from the formal system, though this interpretation can’t be confirmed directly from the data.
  • took.ACT, ACTCompositeScore.filled, and especially sat.taken all showed dramatically stronger relationships here than in the non-sustained sequence — sat.taken in particular flipped from completely null (p = 0.542) to strongly significant (OR ≈ 1.99–2.00) once no-record status was the outcome. This is the single cleanest confirmation of the entire mechanism-split hypothesis in this whole two-part analysis: SAT-taking’s dramatic bivariate disadvantage — the largest raw gap found anywhere in the earlier work — is now confirmed, in a fully controlled multivariate model, to be almost entirely about disappearing from the employment record.
  • attended.ps was strongly protective (OR ≈ 0.61), and notably stronger here than in the non-sustained sequence (OR ≈ 0.67 there) — confirming and sharpening the bivariate finding that this variable’s benefit splits nearly evenly between both mechanisms with a slight edge toward no-record status.
  • total.cte.courses.taken was significant here (unlike its null result in the non-sustained sequence), confirming the earlier three-way bivariate finding that its relationship with disconnection concentrates more in the no-record dimension. cte.achievement’s two categories remained protective in both sequences, confirming CTE’s dual-mechanism role.

The one clear exception: Black graduates

Black graduates showed a significant, protective association in Model 1 (OR = 0.82) — a genuine reversal from the raw bivariate cross-tab, which showed a modestly elevated no-record rate for this group. This protective effect weakened to non-significance once HS characteristics were added in Model 2 and remained null through Models 3 and 4. This should be reported honestly as an unstable, largely null finding for this group and mechanism specifically — distinct from the group’s strong, non-attenuating disadvantage in the non-sustained sequence, and distinct from the consistent, non-attenuating Black-White gap seen throughout the rest of this project’s outcomes.

A finding that didn’t survive full control: PSEO

PSEO participation showed real, independent bivariate relationships with both non-sustained employment and no-record status when tested separately in the earlier three-way comparison. In this fully controlled multivariate model, however, pseo.participant was not significant in any of the four no-record models (nor in the parallel non-sustained sequence). This is worth reporting as exactly the kind of result this confirmatory analysis was designed to catch: a real bivariate relationship that doesn’t survive once demographics, HS context, and other accomplishments are all controlled simultaneously.

A reproducible cross-sequence pattern worth highlighting

avg.unemp.rate emerged as significant and protective once accomplishments were added in Model 3, then dropped back to non-significance once attended.ps was added in Model 4 — and this exact same pattern (emerge in Model 3, disappear in Model 4) also occurred independently in the parallel non-sustained sequence. This consistency across both otherwise-separate models adds confidence that it reflects a real, reproducible dynamic (unemployment’s apparent effect substantially running through postsecondary attendance) rather than noise specific to either individual model.

Bottom line

This sequence delivered the strongest, cleanest confirmatory results of this entire two-part analysis. Nearly every demographic and accomplishment variable behaved exactly as the bivariate mechanism-split hypothesis predicted, with SAT-taking’s complete reversal (null for non-sustained, strongly significant for no-record) standing out as the single most decisive piece of evidence for treating these as genuinely distinct mechanisms. The two departures from expectation — Black graduates’ unstable, ultimately-null coefficient, and PSEO’s failure to survive full multivariate control in either sequence — are both worth reporting explicitly as honest, informative null results rather than smoothed over.

Description of the Analysis and Findings

This analysis tested, via parallel four-block logistic regression sequences (demographics → high school characteristics → high school accomplishments → postsecondary attempt), whether the two disconnection mechanisms identified in the bivariate work — persistent non-sustained employment and persistent no-record status — remain genuinely distinct once all variables are considered simultaneously. Both models were fit on the same analytic population (N = 27,669 non-college graduates, 2011–2019 cohorts), using the same 17-variable candidate set.

The results confirm the mechanism split clearly, though with more nuance than a simple “two separate systems” story would suggest. Of the 17 variables tested, 13 came back significant for at least one outcome, and — as in the bivariate stage — the pattern of which variable matters for which outcome is largely non-overlapping.

Non-English home is the single strongest and most consequential finding in this entire analysis. It shows the largest coefficient found in either sequence (OR = 2.10 for no-record status) and a smaller but genuinely opposite-signed effect for non-sustained employment (OR = 0.65, protective). This is not a case of one mechanism mattering and the other being noise — both effects are large, stable, and move in opposite directions for the same population.

Gender and English learner status are the cleanest single-mechanism confirmations. Gender is significant and harmful for non-sustained employment in every model and completely null for no-record status in every model. English learner status shows the exact mirror pattern. Neither variable shows any hint of “leaking” into the other mechanism, which is about as clean a confirmation as a variable screening exercise like this can produce.

Two variables — economic status and SpecialEdStatus — matter for both mechanisms, but in genuinely different ways. Economic status is harmful for non-sustained employment and protective for no-record status — opposite directions for the same variable. SpecialEdStatus is harmful for both, but far more so for non-sustained employment, and its no-record effect weakens noticeably as accomplishments are added, while its non-sustained effect does not move at all.

CTE engagement is a genuine dual-mechanism finding, not a single-mechanism one as the earlier bivariate work might have suggested. Both cte.achievement categories are significantly protective for both outcomes at similar magnitude. total.cte.courses.taken, however, resolves cleanly to a no-record-only finding — protective and significant there, but not significant for non-sustained employment. This distinction between the categorical and continuous CTE measures is worth carrying into any final report, since treating “CTE” as a single undifferentiated protective factor would obscure this split.

ACT composite score is the analysis’s clearest example of a relationship that emerged only once other variables were controlled. The bivariate analysis found this variable had no significant relationship with non-sustained employment at all (p = 0.393). Once demographics, HS context, and other accomplishments are held constant, it becomes clearly significant and harmful for non-sustained employment (OR = 1.025) — a textbook illustration of why this project’s confirmatory stage exists. Combined with took.ACT’s protective effect (participation helps, but a higher score among test-takers is a modest risk factor), this “engagement helps, performance hurts” tension now replicates across both mechanisms.

Two bivariate findings did not survive full multivariate control. pseo.participant showed real, independent relationships with both mechanisms at the bivariate stage but is not significant for either outcome in any of the eight models here. ap.exam showed a significant bivariate relationship with no-record status specifically, but that relationship disappears once the full variable set is included. Both are worth reporting explicitly as null results rather than smoothed over, since a confirmatory analysis that only reports what replicates isn’t doing its job.

attended.ps and avg.unemp.rate both show a genuine cross-mechanism parallel worth flagging on its own. attended.ps is strongly protective for both outcomes, with a slightly larger effect for no-record status — directly confirming the bivariate finding that this was the one variable operating through both channels roughly equally. avg.unemp.rate, meanwhile, becomes significant for both outcomes once accomplishments are added in Model 3, then loses significance for both once attended.ps enters in Model 4 — the identical pattern occurring independently in two separately-run models, which is a strong indication this reflects a real, reproducible dynamic (unemployment’s apparent effect substantially running through postsecondary attendance) rather than coincidence in either individual sequence.

The Story the Data Tells

Coming into this analysis, the working assumption — built from the bivariate stage — was that “disconnection” among non-college graduates might really be two problems wearing one label. This multivariate confirmation supports that assumption, but with an important refinement: it isn’t a clean, symmetric split where each variable belongs entirely to one mechanism or the other. A meaningful subset of variables — non-English home, economic status, SpecialEdStatus, attended.ps, took.ACT, and ACTCompositeScore — matter for both mechanisms, sometimes in the same direction and sometimes in opposite ones. The real finding isn’t “two separate systems with no overlap.” It’s that combining these two outcomes into a single measure would not just dilute several of these relationships — for non-English home and economic status specifically, it would actively cancel them out, since the same variable pushes in opposite directions depending on which mechanism is being measured.

If you are a non-completer from a non-English-speaking home, the data says both risks apply to you, but very unevenly: you face a dramatically elevated risk of disappearing from Minnesota’s employment record, even as you face a somewhat lower risk of unstable-but-visible employment once you’re in the labor market at all. If you have a documented special education status, both risks apply too, but the instability risk is considerably larger and more durable than the record-disappearance risk, which fades noticeably once your high school accomplishments are accounted for. If your risk factor is gender or English-learner status specifically, only one mechanism applies to you at all — the other shows no relationship whatsoever, in either direction.

This has a direct, practical implication for anyone designing an intervention around this population. Programs built around CTE course completion levels would show real, roughly equal protective results against both instability and disappearance. Programs built around CTE course volume specifically would show results only against disappearance from the record, with no measurable effect on job stability. Programs built around postsecondary attendance — even incomplete attendance — would help with both, more so than almost anything else tested. And any intervention built around a single, undifferentiated theory of “workforce disconnection” risks helping one population while doing essentially nothing for the other, or helping both populations through entirely different, non-substitutable mechanisms that need to be built and evaluated separately from the start.
